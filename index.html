About

My work examines religion and politics in the Middle East by bringing together political sociology, Islamic intellectual history, and critical approaches to coloniality. I am particularly interested in how Islam interacts with political authority, economic transformation, state formation, and struggles over sovereignty and belonging.

My current research develops a new political-sociological account of the formation of political Islam and its relationship to ascetic traditions and modern capitalism. By tracing the transmission and transformation of religious ideas, ethical practices, and political forms, I examine how political Islam emerged through changing relationships among religious authority, state power, and economic life in the modern Middle East.

My historical research focuses particularly on the development of asceticism and Sufism in medieval Iran, with attention to regional traditions, religious networks, intellectual exchange, and the social settings in which mystical ideas and practices emerged. Alongside this work, I study Yarsan religious history and the broader place of minoritized religious traditions in the history of Iran and Kurdistan.

I received my PhD in Iranian Studies from the University of Göttingen, where I specialized in the history of religion. From 2016 to 2024, I taught in the Department of Sociology at Soran University. From 2024 to 2026, I was a Postdoctoral Fellow in the Department of Religious Studies at The University of Texas at Austin. I am currently a Research Fellow in the Southwest Asia and North Africa Program at Cornell University.

I also serve on the Editorial Board of TISHK Magazine and on the Academic Advisory Board of TISHK, Center for Kurdistan Studies.
