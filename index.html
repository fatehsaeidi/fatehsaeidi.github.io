About

I am a historian of Islam and Sufism whose research lies at the intersection of religious studies, history, and politics. My work examines how religious ideas and traditions become historical, social, and political forces.

My research focuses on the development of asceticism, Sufism, and Islamic intellectual traditions in medieval Iran, as well as the relationships among religion, political authority, coloniality, nationalism, and cultural production in the modern Middle East.

My geographical areas of specialization include Iran, Kurdistan, Anatolia, and the broader Persianate and Middle Eastern worlds.

Research

My research is organized around four interconnected areas that examine the historical relationships among religion, culture, political power, and social transformation.

1. Sufism and Islamic Intellectual History

This area examines the historical development of asceticism, classical Sufism, and Islamic intellectual traditions in medieval Iran and the broader Persianate world. My research explores the formation of early Sufi communities, mystical literature, devotional practices, and the relationships among Sufism, theology, philosophy, and social life.

My current book project investigates the development of renunciation and classical Sufism in the Jibāl region, with particular attention to cities such as Iṣfahān, Hamadān, Rayy, and other important intellectual and religious centers of western Iran.

2. Religion and Politics in the Modern Middle East

My work in this area investigates the relationship between religion, political authority, nationalism, violence, and state formation in the modern Middle East. I am particularly interested in the political uses of Islamic discourse, the production of religious legitimacy, sectarian politics, and competing visions of Islamic government and political community.

This research also examines how religious concepts are reinterpreted in response to revolution, war, political mobilization, and struggles over sovereignty and belonging.

3. Coloniality, Culture, and Kurdish Studies

This research examines coloniality, cultural domination, nationalism, and knowledge production in Kurdistan. Drawing on anti-colonial theory, historical sociology, and cultural studies, I explore the relationships among Kurdish identity, religion, state power, language, and the politics of cultural representation.

I am especially interested in the ways colonial and national institutions represent, folklorize, assimilate, or exclude Kurdish communities, as well as the intellectual and cultural practices through which Kurdish actors resist these processes and produce alternative forms of knowledge.

4. Islam, Capitalism, and Political Modernity

This emerging long-term research project investigates the historical formation of modern political Islam in the Middle East. It asks how the aspiration to revive Islamic civilization contributed to the development of modern Islamist movements and how religious revival became connected to political organization, nationalism, economic transformation, and modern state power.

Bringing Weberian historical sociology into dialogue with Marxist social theory, the project examines how selected traditions of asceticism, ethical discipline, and Sufism were transformed and incorporated into different forms of political Islam. It explores how religious discipline, activism, education, entrepreneurship, and communal organization interacted with the development of capitalist social relations.

The project also considers how Islamist movements combined religious universalism with different forms of nationalism. Its comparative cases include Salafism in Saudi Arabia, the Muslim Brotherhood and its networks in the Arab world, including Qatar, Gülenism in Turkey, and modern Shiʿi political Islam and Khomeinism in Iran.

Rather than treating political Islam as external to modernity, this project approaches it as one of the forces through which political, cultural, and capitalist modernity has been produced and transformed in the Middle East.

Teaching

My teaching interests include Islam and politics, the history of Sufism, Islamic intellectual history, religion and politics in the Middle East, sociology of religion, Kurdish cultural studies, colonial and anti-colonial theory, nationalism, and historical sociology.

Current Projects

The Development of Renunciation and Classical Sufism in the Jibāl Region

This book project reconstructs the historical development of asceticism and classical Sufism in western and central Iran. It examines regional Sufi communities, intellectual networks, institutions, texts, practices, and relationships between local traditions and the broader history of Islamic mysticism.

Islam, Capitalism, and Political Modernity

This multi-year comparative project examines the historical rise and transformation of political Islam through the relationships among religious revival, ethical discipline, nationalism, capitalist development, and political power.
